## 1. Background

### 1.a Multi-Agent System Work In Finance

> [paper section] This section keeps five finance multi-agent or
> market-microstructure papers as the conceptual spine. The first three explain
> heterogeneous agent interaction. The last two explain how orders, liquidity,
> volume, and book gaps become price movement.

#### Brock And Hommes 1998

> [AMA citation] Brock WA, Hommes CH. Heterogeneous beliefs and routes to chaos
> in a simple asset pricing model. J Econ Dyn Control. 1998;22(8-9):1235-1274.
> doi:10.1016/S0165-1889(98)00011-6

> [core argument]
>
> - Market behavior can be generated by an ecology of forecasting rules, not by
>   one representative rational agent.
> - The key mechanism is adaptive selection: traders compare rule performance
>   and move capital or attention toward rules that recently worked.
> - When rule-switching becomes strong enough, the market can move from stable
>   adjustment into nonlinear instability and chaos.

> [project consequence]
>
> - `TradingStyle` should be a first-class object, not a string label.
> - Agent design needs performance memory, switching pressure, and the ability
>   to revise belief rules over time.

#### Lux And Marchesi 1999

> [AMA citation] Lux T, Marchesi M. Scaling and criticality in a stochastic
> multi-agent model of a financial market. Nature. 1999;397(6719):498-500.
> doi:10.1038/17290

> [core argument]
>
> - Stylized facts such as fat tails and volatility clustering can emerge
>   endogenously from agent interaction.
> - External news does not need to carry all explanatory weight; population
>   composition and switching dynamics can produce complex market regimes.
> - The market should be treated as a coupled social-price system, not only a
>   data series to be predicted.

> [project consequence]
>
> - Historical K-lines are the base environment, but the simulator should
>   eventually allow agent interaction to alter liquidity, volatility, and
>   regime state.
> - Evaluation must separate exogenous replay effects from endogenous effects
>   created by the agent population.

#### Cont And Bouchaud 2000

> [AMA citation] Cont R, Bouchaud JP. Herd behavior and aggregate fluctuations
> in financial markets. Macroecon Dyn. 2000;4(2):170-196.
> doi:10.1017/S1365100500015029

> [core argument]
>
> - Correlated action can arise from communication, imitation, and random
>   cluster formation.
> - Large price moves can be produced when many locally connected agents make
>   aligned decisions at the same time.
> - Heavy-tailed aggregate returns can therefore be a structural consequence of
>   social clustering, not only a response to large outside shocks.

> [project consequence]
>
> - The future simulator needs an explicit communication or influence layer.
> - `InteractionLog` should preserve whether correlated decisions came from
>   shared data, shared strategy, imitation, or direct communication.

#### Farmer Et Al 2004

> [AMA citation] Farmer JD, Gillemot L, Lillo F, Mike S, Sen A. What really
> causes large price changes? Quant Finance. 2004;4(4):383-397.
> doi:10.1080/14697680400008627

> [core argument]
>
> - Large price changes are not explained mainly by large trade volume.
> - The immediate mechanism is liquidity discreteness: a market order changes
>   price when it consumes the best quote and exposes a gap before the next
>   available quote.
> - Volume matters through the book state it meets. The same order size can
>   produce a small or large tick movement depending on depth, gaps, and current
>   liquidity.

> [project consequence]
>
> - A generative simulator cannot jump directly from agent decision to OHLCV
>   candle. It needs an intermediate order-book world where price ticks are
>   consequences of queue depletion and liquidity gaps.
> - `volume` should eventually be modeled as executed order flow, not only a
>   stored field on `Kline`.
> - Future objects probably need to include `LimitOrderBook`, `BookLevel`,
>   `Order`, `Trade`, `LiquidityGap`, and `PriceTickEvent`.

#### McGroarty Et Al 2019

> [AMA citation] McGroarty F, Booth A, Gerding E, Chinthalapati VLR. High
> frequency trading strategies, market fragility and price spikes: an agent
> based model perspective. Ann Oper Res. 2019;282(1):217-244.
> doi:10.1007/s10479-018-3019-4

> [core argument]
>
> - A richer artificial market can combine multiple agent species inside a
>   functioning limit order book: market makers, fundamental traders,
>   high-frequency momentum traders, high-frequency mean-reversion traders, and
>   noise traders.
> - The model reproduces several empirical microstructure regularities,
>   including clustered volatility, return autocorrelation, long memory in order
>   flow, concave price impact, and extreme price events.
> - Market fragility is not only a property of one bad strategy. It depends on
>   the ecology of strategy types, their timescales, and how aggressively they
>   consume or supply liquidity.

> [project consequence]
>
> - Project 009 should treat `Agent`, `TradingStyle`, `Strategy`, and
>   `OrderBook` as collaborating objects, not as one flattened trading rule.
> - Agent timescale should be explicit. A slow fundamentalist, a Chan-structure
>   reader, a market maker, and a fast momentum agent should not be forced into
>   the same event clock.
> - Later simulation tests should validate not only profit but also
>   microstructure shape: spread, depth, order-flow memory, price impact, volume
>   concentration, and extreme tick events.

### 1.b Chan Theory Concepts And Connections

> [source] This section is based on `memo0012`, Appendix B of `memo0001`, the
> [KWX/CC CSV-to-ZouShi page](https://www.kwx.me/cc/), the Python code blocks
> embedded on that page, the local preserved record under
> `records/cc/kline-to-zoushi-001/`, and the original 缠中说禅 lessons linked
> from the page.

> [source hierarchy] For Project 009 implementation, the KWX/CC page and
> `memo0012` are the reproduction target. Original 缠中说禅 material is used to
> verify important theory rules. The embedded Python code is used to understand
> executable behavior, edge cases, hidden defaults, and checkpoint expectations.
> If website text, original theory, and code diverge, Project 009 should record
> an explicit decision before implementing the rule.

> [concept] Chan theory should be modeled as a deterministic `MarketView`. It
> reads market facts, applies a fixed `ChanConfig`, emits immutable structure
> objects, preserves trace evidence, and passes checkpoint counts or hashes. The
> object chain is not just visual. It is a state machine from raw bars to
> semantic market structure.

> [chain] Checked implementation chain:
>
> ```text
> CSV row
> -> Kline
> -> KlineTSeries
> -> KlineCombiner -> MergedKline
> -> detect_fenxings -> Fenxing
> -> build_bis -> Bi
> -> build_xianduans -> Xianduan
> -> legs_from_xianduans -> build_zhongshus -> ZhongShu
> -> build_l1_zoushis -> CompletedZouShi
> -> canonical_renderer -> SVG
> ```

> [source conflict] The website tutorial checker expects a canonical
> eight-column CSV: `symbol`, `period`, `timestamp`, `open`, `high`, `low`,
> `close`, and `volume`. The local preserved record has two extra helper
> datetime columns and a different CSV SHA. This should be handled by an
> adapter. It should not weaken the canonical K-line contract.

#### CSV Row To Kline（原始K线）

> [concept] `Kline` is the first legal market fact. In the website
> implementation it is a frozen, slotted dataclass with `symbol`, `period`,
> `timestamp`, `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, and `volume`. The fuller data
> model also leaves optional room for `amount`, `open_interest`, and
> `settlement`.

> [derivation] The CSV path is strict. `load_csv()` reads rows, and
> `normalize_rows()` turns a row into `Kline` only after exact-header, non-empty
> symbol/period, integer timestamp, strictly increasing timestamp, finite OHLCV,
> `low <= open/close <= high`, nonnegative volume, one-symbol, and one-period
> checks pass.

> [failure rule] Bad input stops the run with a row-level error. The code does
> not sort, fill defaults, drop rows, resample, or silently repair malformed
> data.

> [project delta] Project 009's experiment `Kline` also carries `source`, with
> empty source normalized to `NA`. Keep that field for data-point completeness,
> but adapters must map website-style CSV rows into the Project 009 field set
> explicitly.

> [checkpoint] The tutorial fixture produces `10,060` legal `Kline` objects.
> Later Chan objects must be traceable back to these raw bars, so `Kline` is
> more than chart input. It is the evidence base.

#### Kline To KlineTSeries（K线时间序列）

> [code observation] The KWX/CC rebuild code passes `list[Kline]` directly into
> `pipeline.build()`. It does not define a separate `KlineTSeries` class.

> [project decision] Project 009 keeps `KlineTSeries` as a wrapper around that
> ordered sequence because the sequence has invariants of its own: one
> symbol/period assumption, strict timestamp order, duplicate rejection,
> start/end timestamps, count, sources, and adapter-friendly tuple export.

> [boundary] This layer still does not interpret Chan structure. It owns
> market-fact sequence validity only. The first Chan rule begins in
> `KlineCombiner`.

> [verification implication] When comparing with KWX/CC output,
> `KlineTSeries.klines` should be able to feed a website-equivalent build
> pipeline without changing order, row count, OHLCV values, period, or symbol.

#### KlineTSeries To MergedKline（合并K线）

> [concept] `MergedKline` is the first Chan-structure object. Website fields are
> `period`, `dir`, `high`, `low`, `start_ts`, `end_ts`, `start_index`,
> `end_index`, and `trace`.

> [direction rule] `KlineCombiner` establishes direction only from strict
> movement. The next unit is `UP` only when both high and low are strictly
> higher. It is `DOWN` only when both are strictly lower. Equality, containment,
> and ambiguity do not establish direction.

> [inclusion rule] Containment is interval containment and includes equal
> boundaries. Once direction exists, the combiner either merges the new raw
> K-line into the last unit or starts a new unit.

> [merge rule] Under upward context, the merged interval keeps
> `high = max(highs)` and `low = max(lows)`. Under downward context, it keeps
> `high = min(highs)` and `low = min(lows)`.

> [algorithm direction] The merge direction is chronological: left to right. If
> the newly merged K-line still contains, or is contained by, the next raw
> K-line, the algorithm compares the merged object with that next raw K-line and
> continues. This can be described recursively, but Project 009 should implement
> it as an iterative left fold or stack pass so the time context remains
> explicit. Right-to-left merging is not acceptable because it changes the
> direction context.

> [bootstrap rule] Before direction is known, the combiner holds a leading
> buffer. The first non-contained, strictly directional pair establishes
> direction. The earlier buffered inclusion chain then collapses into the first
> merged unit under that direction.

> [incremental rule] Same-timestamp tail update is handled by
> `pop_last_kline()`, which can remove the latest raw bar, recompute the last
> unit, and fall back to the undirected leading buffer if the
> direction-establishing bar is removed.

> [checkpoint] The tutorial conversion turns `10,060` raw K-lines into `6,200`
> merged K-lines. Merge first, then detect turning points; fractals detected on
> raw included candles can be false structure.

#### MergedKline To Fenxing（分型）

> [concept] `Fenxing` is a confirmed local turning structure. Website fields are
> `period`, `fx_type`, `index`, `timestamp`, `high`, `low`, and `trace`.

> [derivation] `detect_fenxings()` scans every three-merged-K-line window. The
> middle unit is a top only if both its high and low are strictly higher than
> both neighbors. It is a bottom only if both its high and low are strictly
> lower than both neighbors. Equality is rejected.

> [state] The first and last merged units cannot be confirmed because they lack
> a full three-unit window. The `index` is the middle merged-unit index.
> `timestamp` is the middle merged-unit start time. The object `high` and `low`
> preserve the full three-window extreme range, not only one endpoint price.

> [checkpoint] The tutorial sequence produces `3,027` Fenxing objects from
> `6,200` merged K-lines. `Bi` then uses qualified structural turns, not every
> visible high and low.

#### Fenxing To Bi（笔）

> [concept] `Bi` is the lowest semantic swing unit. Website fields are `period`,
> `direction`, `start_ts`, `end_ts`, `start_price`, `end_price`, `high`, `low`,
> and `trace`.

> [derivation] `build_bis()` consumes merged K-lines, Fenxing objects, raw
> K-lines, and `ChanConfig`. First-bi formation uses a candidate pool until an
> opposite fractal can pass the standard gates. After that, same-type fractals
> extend the current stroke only if they create a more extreme top or bottom;
> opposite-type fractals attempt a new stroke.

> [gates] A standard `Bi` requires opposite fractal types, distance, endpoint,
> and interval-extreme checks. The default website profile uses merged-center
> span with `bi_min_span = 4`, allows real gaps to count as extra bar distance,
> uses `HALF` endpoint check, requires the endpoint to be the true interval
> extreme, and enables fractal repair.

> [raw dependency] `Bi` still needs raw K-line evidence after merging.
> `_has_gap()` looks through raw extremes of adjacent merged units to decide
> whether a true price gap contributes to distance.

> [repair rule] `_repair_sub_peak()` can absorb a false rebound only after a
> bounded future scan proves the newer fractal is the true extreme before a
> valid reverse stroke forms. This repair is an engineering variant in the
> website implementation, not a simple original-text rule.

> [state] Confirmed/forming status is not a field on `Bi`; it is derived from
> trace dependency watermarks, current merged count, and terminal instability
> rules.

> [checkpoint] The tutorial sequence produces `502` Bi objects from `3,027`
> Fenxing objects, including `14` false-rebound repairs, `501` confirmed
> strokes, and one forming tail.

#### Bi To Xianduan（线段）

> [concept] `Xianduan` is a larger swing made from multiple `Bi` objects.
> Website fields are `period`, `direction`, `start_ts`, `end_ts`, `start_price`,
> `end_price`, `high`, `low`, and `trace`.

> [derivation] `build_xianduans()` consumes the Bi sequence and normalized
> `ChanConfig`, then runs a feature-sequence builder. A candidate segment is not
> yet a frozen segment.

> [hard gates] The basic gate requires at least three Bi, an odd member count,
> first and last Bi in the segment direction, and overlap among the first three
> Bi price intervals. Endpoint extreme validity is also required unless the
> candidate is explicitly marked as a project variant.

> [feature sequence] For an upward segment, the feature sequence reads downward
> Bi. For a downward segment, it reads upward Bi. The code treats the second and
> third feature elements differently for inclusion handling, tracks gaps, and
> uses actual-break logic before accepting a feature-sequence fractal.

> [freeze machine] The freeze machine keeps frozen history, `soft_n0`, and
> `pending_n1`. `N0` is a quasi-complete candidate, `N1` is a reverse candidate,
> and a later `N2` or gap-confirmed evidence freezes history. A single break
> does not automatically finish a segment.

> [state] Frozen history is not rewritten. Right-edge provisional segments
> remain visible but must not be consumed as formal center input.

> [checkpoint] The tutorial sequence produces `69` Xianduan objects from `502`
> Bi objects: `67` frozen, one `soft_n0`, and one `pending_n1`.

#### Xianduan To ZhongShu（中枢）

> [concept] `ZhongShu` is an overlapping structural price zone. Website fields
> include `source_type`, `direction`, `zg`, `zd`, `gg`, `dd`, source
> indexes/timestamps, `core_member_ids`, `extended_member_ids`, `member_count`,
> `extension_state`, `mode`, entry evidence, close evidence, and `trace`.

> [project variant] Website L1 centers use frozen `Xianduan` as an
> approximation for lower-level completed trends. The page states this is a
> project variant, not the strict original definition.

> [derivation] `legs_from_xianduans()` selects the longest frozen Xianduan
> prefix and projects it into center legs. `build_zhongshus()` splits the input
> into continuous alternating runs and refuses to build across gaps.

> [E + A/B/C rule] The center construction is `E + A/B/C`. `E` is entry
> evidence. `A`, `B`, and `C` are the three core members. `E` must touch the core
> overlap, but `E` is not a core member.

> [core formula]
>
> ```text
> ZD = max(A.low, B.low, C.low)
> ZG = min(A.high, B.high, C.high)
> ```

> [extension rule] A center exists only when `ZD <= ZG`. Closed-interval
> touching is valid. After core formation, `ZD/ZG` remain fixed. Later
> same-position extension checks add pairs of legs only if the candidate leg
> overlaps fixed `[ZD, ZG]`; extension updates outer `GG/DD`, not the core.

> [close rule] The first later leg that does not overlap fixed `[ZD, ZG]`
> becomes close evidence, not a member. Exit direction is up when its low is
> above `ZG`, otherwise down because the non-overlap must be below.

> [restart rule] After closure, the restart logic uses the close evidence and
> adjacent prior leg to choose the next `E`, and it carries the required entry
> direction forward.

> [checkpoint] The tutorial sequence produces `9` segment-level ZhongShu
> objects from `69` Xianduan objects: `8` closed and one open.

#### ZhongShu To ZouShi（走势）

> [concept] `CompletedZouShi` is the larger market movement described through
> official centers and source legs. Website fields include `level`,
> `source_type`, `kind`, `trend_direction`, start/end endpoints, source member
> ids, ZhongShu ids, completion state, close evidence, and trace.

> [input discipline] `build_l1_zoushis()` adapts the standard engine's longest
> frozen Xianduan prefix into `LevelLeg` objects, then consumes the already
> official segment-level ZhongShu list. The ZouShi builder rejects mismatched
> level, source type, period, mode, or ordering. It must not rescan or replace
> official centers silently.

> [relation rule] Two centers form an upward trend relation only when the later
> `DD` is above the earlier `GG`. They form a downward relation only when the
> later `GG` is below the earlier `DD`. Other overlap patterns become expansion
> or undetermined, not trend by slope.

> [kernel rule] The kernel splits continuous source runs, tracks center
> positions, builds forming or frozen slices, and preserves real lifecycle
> high/low through source-leg extreme references.

> [boundary rule] A forming ZouShi has no close evidence. A frozen ZouShi must
> have exactly one close evidence, either a center or a source leg, and that
> evidence cannot also be a member. Adjacent completed trends may share the same
> boundary timestamp and price.

> [checkpoint] The tutorial sequence produces `5` L1 ZouShi objects from `9`
> segment-level centers: four frozen objects and one forming object. The latest
> forming L1 object has `20` member segments, `4` centers, and `2` tail segments
> not yet absorbed.

#### ZouShi To Renderer / SVG（图形输出）

> [concept] The renderer is the visual verification surface. It consumes the
> object chain and a fixed display window. It should not rediscover Chan
> structure. Algorithm objects decide what exists; renderer code decides how the
> same objects become deterministic SVG bytes.

> [window] The website's shared display window is raw index `[2427:2677]`,
> Beijing time `2025-04-07 09:00` to `2025-04-21 22:00`, `250` bars, and price
> range `3004` to `3168`. The local saved record's story SVG uses a different
> 720-bar presentation window, so visual comparisons must name which artifact
> is being checked.

> [verification] If a rendered SVG differs from the reference, the project
> should record whether the error came from raw data parsing, containment merge,
> fractal detection, stroke validation, segment confirmation, center overlap,
> trend interpretation, or drawing coordinates.

> [boundary] Do not put Chan rules inside `Kline` or `KlineTSeries`. Those two
> classes are market facts. Chan structures belong in a later `MarketView` or
> structure layer.

> [next design target] The immediate next Chan-related design target is
> `MergedKline` plus `KlineCombiner`, not the whole chain.

### 1.c Classic And Latest AI Multi-Agent System Trends

> [paper section] This section keeps AI multi-agent system papers in the same
> shape as `1.a`: one paper at a time, citation first, then core argument and
> Project 009 consequence. The classic papers define coordination, autonomy,
> and belief/intent separation. The modern papers add LLM memory, role
> orchestration, tool use, evaluation, and governance.

#### Smith 1980

> [AMA citation] Smith RG. The contract net protocol: high-level communication
> and control in a distributed problem solver. IEEE Trans Comput.
> 1980;C-29(12):1104-1113. doi:10.1109/TC.1980.1675516

> [core argument]
>
> - Classical MAS starts from distributed coordination, not from a single
>   centralized decision maker.
> - The Contract Net protocol makes task announcement, bidding, selection, and
>   execution explicit.
> - The important abstraction is local competence plus social allocation of
>   work; "many agents" is not enough unless the coordination protocol is
>   visible.

> [project consequence]
>
> - Project 009 should eventually model interaction protocols explicitly rather
>   than hiding coordination inside one `Agent.run()` method.
> - Future objects may include `InteractionProtocol`, `TaskAnnouncement`,
>   `AgentBid`, and `InteractionLog`.

#### Wooldridge And Jennings 1995

> [AMA citation] Wooldridge M, Jennings NR. Intelligent agents: theory and
> practice. Knowl Eng Rev. 1995;10(2):115-152.
> doi:10.1017/S0269888900008122

> [core argument]
>
> - An agent is defined by autonomy, reactivity, pro-activeness, and social
>   ability.
> - Agency is therefore not the same thing as calling a prediction model; it
>   requires a durable boundary between the actor, its environment, its goals,
>   and its interactions.
> - This paper remains useful because it separates "intelligent behavior" from
>   the software responsibilities needed to host that behavior.

> [project consequence]
>
> - `AgentProfile` should record more than a strategy name.
> - It should eventually include role, goals, allowed actions, observation
>   style, time horizon, social behavior, and constraints.

#### Rao And Georgeff 1995

> [AMA citation] Rao AS, Georgeff MP. BDI agents: from theory to practice. In:
> Proceedings of the First International Conference on Multiagent Systems. AAAI
> Press; 1995.

> [core argument]
>
> - Belief, desire, and intention give a clean decomposition of agent cognition.
> - Beliefs describe the agent's world model; desires describe objectives;
>   intentions describe committed plans or courses of action.
> - The useful lesson is commitment under uncertainty: an agent should not
>   recompute from zero on every tick as if it has no memory or prior plan.

> [project consequence]
>
> - `AgentMemory`, `StrategyBook`, `DecisionSignal`, `OrderIntent`, and actual
>   execution should remain separate objects.
> - A future trading agent can hold beliefs about market views, desires about
>   risk/return, and intentions about position plans without collapsing them
>   into one method call.

#### Park Et Al 2023

> [AMA citation] Park JS, O'Brien J, Cai CJ, Morris MR, Liang P, Bernstein MS.
> Generative agents: interactive simulacra of human behavior. In: Proceedings of
> the 36th Annual ACM Symposium on User Interface Software and Technology. ACM;
> 2023:Article 2, 1-22. doi:10.1145/3586183.3606763

> [core argument]
>
> - Believable LLM agents require observation, memory, reflection, and planning
>   inside a simulated world.
> - The agent is not only the language model call; it is the surrounding memory
>   and world-state machinery that lets behavior persist across time.
> - Socially coherent behavior emerges when agents can perceive events, store
>   experience, retrieve relevant memories, and plan actions from that context.

> [project consequence]
>
> - Project 009 should not treat a stochastic or LLM-based trader as a stateless
>   signal generator.
> - If LLM agents are added later, they should use durable `AgentMemory`,
>   observation records, reflection summaries, and reproducible action traces.

#### Wu Et Al 2023

> [AMA citation] Wu Q, Bansal G, Zhang J, et al. AutoGen: enabling next-gen LLM
> applications via multi-agent conversation. arXiv. Published August 16, 2023.
> Revised October 3, 2023. doi:10.48550/arXiv.2308.08155

> [core argument]
>
> - Modern LLM-MAS work is strongly about orchestration: roles, tools, human
>   input, code execution, and conversation patterns.
> - Multi-agent conversation is useful only when the system controls who can act,
>   what tools they can use, and how outputs feed later actions.
> - The infrastructure around the agents is as important as the prompt inside
>   one agent.

> [project consequence]
>
> - Project 009 should separate `AgentRuntime`, `ToolAdapter`,
>   `ObservationBuilder`, and `DecisionLedger`.
> - Agents should not directly own every external tool or data adapter.

#### Hong Et Al 2024

> [AMA citation] Hong S, Zhuge M, Chen J, et al. MetaGPT: meta programming for
> a multi-agent collaborative framework. In: International Conference on
> Learning Representations; 2024. doi:10.48550/arXiv.2308.00352

> [core argument]
>
> - Free-form agent chat can amplify inconsistency when roles, artifacts, and
>   output standards are weak.
> - MetaGPT's key lesson is not just "many agents"; it is role assignment,
>   standard operating procedures, and structured intermediate products.
> - Multi-agent intelligence improves when collaboration is constrained by
>   durable artifacts rather than unbounded conversation.

> [project consequence]
>
> - Future trading agents should exchange structured objects such as
>   `MarketView`, `DecisionSignal`, `OrderIntent`, and `InteractionLog`.
> - Natural-language reasoning can annotate decisions, but it should not replace
>   typed state and reproducible artifacts.

#### Li Et Al 2024

> [AMA citation] Li X, Wang S, Zeng S, Wu Y, Yang Y. A survey on LLM-based
> multi-agent systems: workflow, infrastructure, and challenges. Vicinagearth.
> 2024;1:9. doi:10.1007/s44336-024-00009-2

> [core argument]
>
> - LLM-MAS should be understood as a lifecycle: profile, perception,
>   self-action, mutual interaction, and evolution.
> - The hard problems are not only prompt quality; they include infrastructure,
>   coordination, scaling, memory, evaluation, and safety.
> - The field is moving from simple agent conversations toward managed systems
>   of roles, environments, actions, and feedback.

> [project consequence]
>
> - Project 009 should name lifecycle objects early even if implementation
>   stays small.
> - Candidate boundaries include `AgentProfile`, `Observation`, `Action`,
>   `Interaction`, `AgentMemory`, and `EvaluationReport`.

#### Liu Et Al 2023

> [AMA citation] Liu X, Yu H, Zhang H, et al. AgentBench: evaluating LLMs as
> agents. arXiv. Published August 7, 2023. Revised October 4, 2025.
> doi:10.48550/arXiv.2308.03688

> [core argument]
>
> - Agent systems need evaluation beyond final text quality.
> - Long-horizon reasoning, action selection, tool use, instruction following,
>   and failure traces must be measured in interactive environments.
> - A convincing agent benchmark tests whether decisions remain coherent across
>   steps, not whether one answer sounds intelligent.

> [project consequence]
>
> - Project 009 needs `DecisionLedger` and `MetricsReport` early in the
>   simulation architecture.
> - Trading-agent claims should be scored by reproducible tasks, action traces,
>   and measurable outcomes, not by narrative quality.

#### Mialon Et Al 2024

> [AMA citation] Mialon G, Fourrier C, Swift C, Wolf T, LeCun Y, Scialom T.
> GAIA: a benchmark for general AI assistants. In: International Conference on
> Learning Representations; 2024.

> [core argument]
>
> - The benchmark trend is toward real-world, multi-step, tool-using tasks with
>   verifiable answers.

### 1.d Computation Resource Estimate

> [scope] Estimate the first realistic simulation target: `10` stocks, `10`
> years, hourly bars, Chan-theory market views, and `100` agents.

> [assumption] US regular-market hourly data is roughly `252` trading days per
> year and about `7` hourly bars per day.
>
> ```text
> market_steps = 10 years * 252 days/year * 7 bars/day = 17,640
> kline_rows = 10 symbols * 17,640 = 176,400
> ```

> [note] If the intended universe is not `10` stocks, replace `10 symbols` with
> the real number. The formula matters more than the first estimate.

Decision-count estimate:

| Mode | Formula | Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Portfolio-step agent | `17,640 market_steps * 100 agents` | `1,764,000` decisions | Each agent sees the whole market snapshot and makes one portfolio-level decision per hour. |
| Per-symbol agent | `176,400 kline_rows * 100 agents` | `17,640,000` decisions | Each agent evaluates every symbol at every hour. |
| Monte Carlo runs | `base decisions * run_count` | `10x`, `100x`, `1000x` growth | Stochastic agent behavior multiplies compute and log volume directly. |

Interaction-count estimate:

> [interaction] Agent interaction is a separate cost from market-data size. If
> agents only consume shared market views, the system is small. If every agent
> communicates with every other agent at every step, the log volume becomes the
> main sizing problem.

| Mode | Formula | Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sparse social graph | `17,640 market_steps * 100 agents * 3-10 peers` | `5.3M-17.6M` messages | Each agent talks to a few neighbors per market step. This is the better first design. |
| Dense all-to-all, portfolio clock | `17,640 market_steps * 100 * 99 / 2` | `87.3M` pair interactions, or `174.6M` directed messages | Every agent can react to every other agent once per market step. |
| Dense all-to-all, per-symbol clock | `176,400 symbol_steps * 100 * 99 / 2` | `873.2M` pair interactions, or `1.746B` directed messages | Much heavier. Avoid until there is a clear reason. |

Layer estimate:

| Layer | Laptop Prototype | Workstation / Larger Batch | Main Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raw hourly OHLCV | Small: CSV/Parquet should stay below a few hundred MB for `10` symbols. | Still easy until the universe becomes thousands of symbols. | Duplicating the same data per agent. |
| `Kline` / `KlineTSeries` objects | Fine on `16 GB` RAM if used carefully. | Better as columnar arrays plus object adapters. | Python object overhead if every bar becomes a heavy object everywhere. |
| Chan structures | Precompute once per symbol; expected to be linear in bars. | Cache by symbol, period, and parameter set. | Recomputing Chan views inside every agent. |
| 100 deterministic agents | Fine for one pass on a modern laptop CPU. | Use multiprocessing or batch workers for many seeds. | Writing huge Python-object logs. |
| 100 LLM agents | Not viable per bar or per decision. | Use sparse LLM calls only for high-level reasoning. | Millions of LLM calls would dominate cost and latency. |
| Event and decision logs | Fine if compact and append-only. | Store as Parquet/JSONL partitions by run. | Logging full snapshots instead of deltas/references. |

Hardware range estimate:

| Target | Memory | Disk / SSD | CPU | GPU | Practical Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum deterministic prototype | `8-16 GB` RAM | `5-20 GB` free | `4-8` cores | Not needed | Works if Chan views are shared and logs are compact. |
| Comfortable 100-agent research loop | `16-32 GB` RAM | `20-100 GB` free SSD | `8-16` cores | Not needed | Good target for local development, repeated runs, plots, and reports. |
| Dense interaction logs or `10-100` Monte Carlo seeds | `32-64 GB` RAM | `100-500 GB` SSD | `16-32` cores | Usually not needed | Needed when storing many interaction events or running many stochastic seeds. |
| Local ML or LLM agents | `64-128 GB` system RAM | `500 GB-2 TB` SSD | `16-32` cores | `16-48 GB+` VRAM if local models are used | Model memory dominates. Do not put local LLM calls inside every bar or every decision. |

> [storage math] Raw OHLCV, feature columns, and Chan structures for this
> `10`-stock hourly scope should usually fit under `1 GB` if stored sanely.
> Compact decision logs can live in the low-GB range. Verbose JSONL, dense
> agent-to-agent messages, full state snapshots, or many Monte Carlo seeds can
> push the run archive into `100 GB+` quickly.

> [essential] For the first 10-stock, 10-year, hourly, 100-agent simulation,
> start with `16-32 GB` RAM, `20-100 GB` free SSD, an `8-16` core CPU, and no
> GPU. The data size is not the hard part. The hard part is avoiding duplicated
> market views, avoiding per-decision LLM calls, and keeping logs compact.

### 2.b Logical Layer Architecture

> [decision] Use the following layer map as the current approved architecture
> spine for Project 009 planning. The first six and last two layers already
> have concrete folders; the middle research/simulation layers are future

The logical design should grow in layers, even before every folder exists:

```text
Reference Evidence      records/
```

Layer notes:

> [reference evidence] `records/` preserves outside cases with provenance. In
> this project, the `cc` K-line-to-ZouShi record lives here so the original CSV,
> manifest, and SVG stay together. It is used for comparison and understanding,
> not as importable source code.

> [sample inputs] `data_samples/` holds tiny checked-in datasets that tests and
> examples can safely reuse. These files should be small, public, and stable
> enough to support repeatable verification.

> [experiment sandbox] `experiments/` is where uncertain ideas are tried before
> they become project source. The first K-line trials have already moved to
> `src`; new uncertain shapes should start here again.

> [domain facts] `src/tradinglab/domain/` is for stable market and trading
> concepts. These classes should be clean, typed, and independent from files,
> Qlib, chart libraries, strategy rules, and external systems.

> [data interface] `src/tradinglab/data_io/` is for reading,
> normalizing, and validating data before it becomes domain objects. CSV
> loading and K-line validation belong here. It is the communication point
> between raw files and Project 009's market-fact objects.

> [integration interface] `src/tradinglab/integrations/` is for external
> libraries and vendor conventions. Qlib adapters belong here so Qlib-specific
> objects do not leak into the core domain. It is the communication point
> between Project 009 and outside systems.

> [market views] Future `src/tradinglab/market_views/` should hold structured
> interpretations of market facts. Chan theory belongs here conceptually:
> `KlineTSeries` in, `MergedKline`, `Fenxing`, `Bi`, `Xianduan`, `ZhongShu`,
> and `ZouShi` out.

> [features] Future `src/tradinglab/features/` should hold reusable numeric or
> symbolic transforms: indicators, factors, volatility measures, volume
> features, and fuzzy confidence inputs. A feature should be reusable by many
> strategies.

> [strategies] Future `src/tradinglab/strategies/` should hold replaceable
> decision policies. A strategy consumes market views and features, then emits
> candidate signals. It should not own agent memory or market execution.

> [agents] Future `src/tradinglab/agents/` should hold actors with identity,
> memory, capital, risk posture, trading style, and adaptation rules. Agents
> use strategies; they are not just strategy functions with names.

> [simulation world] Future `src/tradinglab/simulation/` or `engine/` should
> own time, event order, agent interaction, orders, fills, optional order-book
> state, and world-state updates. This is where replay and generative
> simulation must be kept separate.

> [evaluation] Future `src/tradinglab/evaluation/` should measure what
> happened. It should read evidence from runs and produce metrics, risk
> summaries, attribution, robustness checks, and failure analysis.

> [visualization] `src/tradinglab/visualization/` is for reusable chart
> objects, adapters, renderers, and overlay transforms. The first chart object
> is `KlineTSeriesChart`: one visual view over one `KlineTSeries`. It should
> produce chart-ready views and artifacts, not own the trading rules.

> [generated evidence] `artifacts/` stores generated outputs such as charts,
> reports, run manifests, logs, and comparison results. It is the audit trail
> of experiments, not the place for reusable implementation code.

> [design pressure] The layers are real communication interfaces. The folders
> for future layers should not be created only to make the tree look complete.
> Create them when a milestone needs real classes, tests, and examples.

### 2.d Future Agent And Simulation Architecture

After market facts and market views are trustworthy, the future agent stack can
grow from this chain:

```text
MarketViewCache
-> ObservationBuilder
-> StrategyBook
-> Strategy
-> DecisionSignal
-> Agent
-> AgentRuntime
-> OrderIntent
-> MarketWorld / SimulationClock
-> LimitOrderBook
-> Order / Trade / Fill
-> Position / PortfolioState
-> DecisionLedger / InteractionLog
-> MetricsReport
```

> [market view cache] `MarketViewCache` is shared. Agents should reference
> market views, not copy K-lines, feature arrays, or Chan structures privately.

> [observation] `ObservationBuilder` controls what an agent sees at one time
> step. This keeps experiments fair and makes information access explicit.

> [strategy] `Strategy` is a replaceable policy. It consumes market views and
> features, then emits `DecisionSignal`. Strategy is not agent identity.

> [agent] `Agent` owns profile, memory, cash, positions, risk constraints,
> current intentions, adaptation rules, and `TradingStyle`. It chooses how to
> use strategies; it should not own every detector or adapter.

> [book interaction] Agent-to-agent communication may change beliefs,
> confidence, imitation pressure, and order intent. It should not move price
> directly. Market movement should happen when `OrderIntent` becomes orders
> that hit or rest on a `LimitOrderBook`.

> [key learning 2026-08-31] The important architecture insight is causal:
> agents interact with other agents through belief, confidence, imitation, and
> intent, but they interact with market price through the book. The book is the
> mechanical interface where social behavior becomes trades, volume, liquidity
> gaps, and price ticks.

> [world] `MarketWorld` owns the clock, event order, order handling, fills,
> optional book state, and social interaction. It is the separation line between
> replay backtest and generative market simulation.

> [order book] `LimitOrderBook` owns bids, asks, depth, queue priority,
> matching, liquidity gaps, trades, and fills. Price ticks and volume should
> emerge from book state and order flow, not from agents directly editing the
> K-line.

> [evidence] `DecisionLedger`, `InteractionLog`, and `MetricsReport` preserve
> the causal trail. A simulator is not credible unless decisions can be traced
> back to views, strategies, memory, social influence, and uncertainty.

### 2.e Runtime Loop

The future simulation loop should be explicit:

```text
1. Load sample or external market data.
2. Validate and normalize it into Project 009 domain objects.
3. Build or refresh market views once per symbol and parameter set.
4. At each simulation time step, build each agent's observation.
5. Let each strategy produce candidate decision signals.
6. Let each agent filter signals through style, memory, risk, and position.
7. Let the world submit order intents to the book and resolve trades, fills,
   interactions, and state updates.
8. Write decision and interaction evidence.
9. Produce metrics, charts, and reports from recorded evidence.
```

> [llm rule] Do not put LLM calls inside every bar or every decision.
> Future LLM use should be sparse: strategy review, hypothesis generation,
> explanation, high-level fundamentalist reasoning, or after-run analysis.

### 2.f Dependency Rules

> [rule] Keep deterministic calculation separate from stochastic behavior.
> Chan structures, indicators, and feature transforms should be reproducible.
> Agent choice, social influence, fuzzy confidence, and random behavior should
> be logged as simulation state.

> [rule] Keep IO at the edge. CSV files, databases, chart export, and external
> tools should enter through adapters. Core objects should be testable without
> touching the filesystem or network.

> [rule] Dependency direction should be one-way:
>
> ```text
> domain <- data
> domain <- visualization
> domain <- integrations
> domain <- future market_views/features
> market_views/features <- future strategies
> strategies <- future agents
> agents <- future simulation
> simulation <- future evaluation/reporting
> ```
>
> Lower layers should not import higher layers. `domain/` should stay the
> cleanest layer.

> [rule] Keep the first implementation small. A compact prototype can still fit
> on a normal development laptop: `16 GB` RAM, SSD, no GPU, and one Python
> process if logs stay compact. A comfortable research machine is closer to
> `16-32 GB` RAM and `8-16` CPU cores. Move to worker batches only after adding
> many symbols, many Monte Carlo seeds, dense interaction logs, or local ML
> models.

> [placement rule] The next useful architecture design target should be one small
> class family tied to an existing folder or experiment path. If the class has
> no concrete folder home, it is probably still a memo topic, not source code.

### 2.g Game Design Reading

> [game design] Read Project 009 as a simulation game before reading it as a
> quant library. The market is the world, K-lines are world facts, market views
> are perception systems, strategies are behavior policies, agents are
> controllers, simulation is the game loop, visualization is rendering, and
> artifacts are replay/debug evidence.

The approved architecture spine maps cleanly to a game-style engine:

| Project 009 Layer | Game Design Reading | Design Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reference Evidence | Reference material / captured match replay | Preserved outside examples used to reproduce behavior and compare results. |
| Sample Inputs | Test map / small scenario | Tiny fixed data used to test mechanics without loading the whole world. |
| Experiment Sandbox | Prototype scene | Fast place to try mechanics before deciding they belong in the main engine. |
| Domain Facts | Core world state | Stable facts such as bars, orders, trades, positions, timestamps, and symbols. |
| Data Interface | Content import pipeline | Converts raw external files into clean world-state objects. |
| Integration Interface | Platform adapter | Lets outside systems talk to the engine without owning engine rules. |
| Market Views | Perception system | Converts world facts into structured views an agent can observe. |
| Features | Derived components | Reusable numeric or symbolic signals attached to the world state. |
| Strategies | Behavior policy | Produces candidate actions from observations and derived views. |
| Agents | AI controller / player model | Owns identity, memory, capital, risk, style, and final action choice. |
| Simulation World | Game loop and rules engine | Advances time, resolves actions, applies fills, updates state, and orders events. |
| Evaluation | Telemetry and scoring | Measures run quality, risk, failure modes, and strategy/agent behavior. |
| Visualization | Renderer and debug overlay | Shows world state, candles, Chan structures, decisions, and run evidence. |
| Generated Evidence | Replay file / debug capture | Stores charts, logs, reports, manifests, and comparison outputs. |

> [engine rule] The simulator should own time. Agents should not advance time
> themselves. They receive observations, propose actions, and wait for the
> world to resolve consequences.

> [state rule] Treat market state as shared world state. Agents should hold
> memory, beliefs, positions, and intentions, but should not privately mutate
> the market facts or market views.

> [system rule] Chan detection, indicators, risk checks, order matching, social
> influence, and rendering are separate systems. Each system reads the current
> world state, emits typed results, and leaves an evidence trail.

> [prototype rule] The first useful game-like prototype is not a full trading
> game UI. It is a deterministic replay loop:
>
> ```text
> load sample bars
> -> validate world facts
> -> step the simulation clock
> -> update market views
> -> record observations
> -> render chart/debug overlay
> -> write replay artifacts
> ```

> [design implication] This perspective supports the OOP direction. Classes
> should model objects with persistent roles in the world: `Kline`,
> `KlineTSeries`, `MarketView`, `Strategy`, `Agent`, `MarketWorld`,
> `SimulationClock`, `DecisionLedger`, and `KlineTSeriesChart`. The
> architecture should make their interactions visible instead of hiding them
> inside one large script.
